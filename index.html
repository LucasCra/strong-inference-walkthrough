Two kinds of knowledge, and a limit on a branch

The tree holds two kinds. Problem knowledge says what is true of the problem: exclusions, findings, what holds. The loop record says how good the researcher’s own estimates have been: the expected gain of a question, the size of a prediction, a noise floor, a cost. It is derived, so it is never stale and nobody can tune it, and it outlives the problem, because the next project uses the same instrument. With few runs it holds anecdotes, not ratios, so nothing is tuned on it before fifteen or twenty reviewed runs. A discount can also over-correct until no question seems to reach the objective; the “really trying” rule at the objective gate guards against that.

A recycle can repeat without end: a branch can add rivals as fast as it excludes them, or keep spending on a gain that does not arrive. So the red recycle edge carries the branch gate. It shows three numbers for every open question: rivals added against excluded over the last three runs, realized against predicted gain, and the budget used. Yes sends the cycle back to step 4. No sends the branch to Parked: the best answer is written, the standing rivals are marked untested (never excluded), and the cycle returns to step 3 for another question. Parking is not abandoning, and a parked branch is reopened only by a new finding that bears on it. A free step also cannot block a paid one: a planned analysis that fails is retried once, and dropped with a note if it fails twice. The thresholds are first guesses and are not tuned.

The life of a finding

  1. SeenStep 8 · openThe standing checks show it. It is written with its size and run.
  2. KeptStep 9 · openIt is recorded, whatever the gates decide about the hypotheses.
  3. ReopenedAny time · openChanged conditions turn an old exclusion into a new finding.
  4. DecidedStep 3 · 3 waysRepeats and needed: promote. Repeats, not needed: defer. Does not repeat: drop. A miss measured here needs no repeat.
  5. TestedSteps 4 to 7 · promotedIt becomes a how or why question with rivals and a new experiment.
  6. UsedStep 10 · closedThe surviving explanation changes something, and the change is measured.
  7. RepeatedStep 9, then step 3 · openA miss cause seen three times in the last five misses is logged as a finding about the protocol, then follows the same path.
  8. ReportedStop · unchangedEvery finding is listed with its status, so stopping cannot lose one.

A repeated miss cause is how a repeated miss changes a protocol: it is decided at step 3, promoted into a how or why question about the measurement, tested and used. The count of three in five is a first guess and is not tuned. Three obligations hold this together: step 9 needs a findings entry, even if empty; step 3 needs a decision on each open finding; Stop needs every finding’s status. These are rules of the method. For now they rest on the researcher’s discipline, and nothing checks them.

Left out on purpose

These wait until more real runs show a need.

  • Independent checking: who runs, who judges, who sees what
  • Reusing data across cycles, which can overfit even a holdout set
  • Diverse hypotheses: step 4 marks each pair exclusive or compound, but nothing verifies that rivals really differ
  • Choosing what is worth doing, beyond the first version: step 3 shows gain and cost corrected by the loop record; discounting gain per source and priors by lever type are open
  • Whether the measure captures the objective. A public score is still only a stand-in for the final one
  • Whether all these rules pay for their cost
  • Thresholds and quantities: three misses in five, a quarter of predicted gain and half of the remaining budget are not tuned, and parked branches are not reopened automatically. Both wait for fifteen or twenty reviewed runs. Which standing checks earn their place is also open
  • Recording pruned hypotheses
  • Rivals that predict the same thing: step 4’s marking and step 6’s outcome table should expose them, but both are the researcher’s declaration
  • Three reasons to stop sharing one gate: success, an empty budget and a wrong objective
  • Outside criticism and independent replication

The same notebook as on page one, with the panels a competition adds: the ledger of levers, the pairs of local and public scores, and the levers that are blocked. Nothing is erased. Scrub the timeline and watch it grow.

Lever ledger

A view of the tree: every lever is a question. Public size is what other people measured, under other conditions. Saturation is the gain of the last two runs against twice the family’s noise floor. Blocked levers are listed first, by gain.

Pairs per regime

Every submission yields a pair: the local estimate written before, the public score after. Offset is public minus local. The first pair in a regime is that regime’s calibration. A later pair is inside tolerance when its offset differs from the recorded one by less than the tolerance, and on the edge counts as outside.

Questions and hypotheses

The base’s logical tree, for the lever questions and the puzzle. A lever question holds one hypothesis and the placeholder. A puzzle holds rivals.

Loop record

The base’s five rows, plus two this adaptation adds: the pairs per regime, and the looks per adoption on the dev set. Rebuilt from the records and never edited by hand. Not read for tuning before fifteen or twenty reviewed runs.

Findings

What nobody predicted. A finding suggests a question and supports nothing yet. Select a stage in its trail to jump there.

Blocked levers

A lever whose best estimate needs an asset that does not exist, or what a constraint forbids, is still written. It carries the constraint, the gain, the cost and the proposal that would unblock it.

What a competition changes

The cycle, steps 0 to 10, is the same, and so are its gates, the tree, the path of a finding, the branch gate and the Parked outcome. A competition changes six things about how the cycle is driven. Everything here is inference from the base workflow, from the first real run of the harness and from Kaggle practice, not from the reading notes. Rules marked as first guesses are not tuned.

What changesThe ruleWhere it sits in the base cycle
1. What a competition fixes before step 0The measure is partly hidden: the public board scores a sample of the test set, and prior work carries public scores. Step 0 writes the metric, the target and its margin above the board, the deadline, the hard caps, the submission form and the final-selection rule, written before the first submission and never by the public score alone. Submissions per day and calendar time are watched, not sized against. A plan whose worst case exceeds what remains of a hard cap is cut before it opens.Step 0, and the budget it already fixes.
2. Two loops: levers outside, puzzles insideThe outer loop is a ledger of levers: public size, local size, saturation, cost per point. A lever question has one hypothesis, a prediction with a size and a cost, and the placeholder; its outcome excludes only below the prediction minus the family’s floor, and more seeds decide a result between. A puzzle opens only on a surprise measured here: a missed rung that repeats on a second rung of the same lever, a pair outside tolerance, or a plateau where the public work says there is room. Two lever questions stay open on different levers while the ledger holds two unsaturated levers.Step 3’s gate decides lever or puzzle. The ledger is a view of the tree, rendered beside the loop record.
3. Bootstrapping from public workA person builds a frontier baseline as support work, not a hypothesis, which anchors the ledger. Every public contribution enters the ledger as settled under other conditions: a candidate rung with a size, not knowledge. Its first local run is a transfer test. The reading answers three questions per lever: has someone measured it, what size would it predict here, which levers are missing.Steps 1 and 2, the know-the-field loop. The first local run of a seeded rung is a lever question at step 3.
4. Calibration: the local measurement against the boardEvery submission yields a pair, tagged by family and training regime. The first pair in a regime is its calibration: an offset and a tolerance (first guess 0.03). A later pair inside tolerance confirms the offset for free. A pair outside opens a calibration puzzle with a stock family of rivals. The protocol changes only as the outcome of such a puzzle, never on a single miss. Local decides ranking; the board decides calibration and the final pick. A holdout is scored only when a lever is adopted, and the looks are counted.Two rows added to the loop record, read at steps 3, 5 and 6. The puzzle is a puzzle at step 3. The protocol change is support work, tested by the next pair. The base’s “regime-offset” cause at step 9 records the same thing.
5. Really trying, and what blocks a lever“Objective wrong” fires only when every lever is saturated or blocked, the reading for “no path reaches” has run at least twice since the last lever was added and found nothing, and no open puzzle has budget left. A lever whose best estimate needs an asset that does not exist, or what a constraint forbids, is written as blocked, with its gain, cost and the proposal that would unblock it. For data, the proposal starts with a cheap pilot.The objective gate at the end of the cycle, and step 2 when the reading is run.
6. What the harness’s records holdA lever question is a question with kind lever. The ledger is a rendered table over lever questions. A pair is fields on a submitted experiment, tagged by regime. The offset and tolerance are fields on the phase. A calibration puzzle is a question with kind puzzle and the stock rivals pre-filled. Blocked is a hypothesis status with a constraint and a proposal. The hard caps are the objective’s constraints. The “really trying” test is a check the render runs over the ledger and the reading log.Nothing new in the cycle. This is how the instance is built.

What stays out of the first version

Each of these waits for fifteen or twenty reviewed runs on the instance.

  • Per-regime tolerances other than the first guess
  • An automatic cost-per-point ranking. Whoever picks the next question reads the ledger and says why, as step 3’s discounted gain and corrected cost already require
  • Any portfolio rule beyond keeping two lever questions open
  • Adversarial validation as a standing check. It is worth adding once the holdout exists

What to record to judge this adaptation

These are the numbers that will say whether the adaptation pays for its rules.

  • Points per GPU hour and per dollar, for each lever
  • The pairs of local and public score per regime, and how many fell outside tolerance
  • How many runs were lever runs and how many were puzzle runs
  • How many times “no path reaches” fired, and what followed
  • How many looks at the dev set each adoption took
